# Overview acquisition

By the end of this page you have a tiled image of the grid to navigate by,
have marked where the lamellae go on it, and know how the same place looks
to the ion beam at the two orientations you mill from.

## Before you start

- An [experiment](/docs/experiments) is open.
- The stage is at the **SEM orientation**, over the grid, with the surface
  in [coincidence](/docs/movement#5-coincidence-and-the-vertical-move).
  Overviews are stitched from the stage positions of their tiles, so a
  height error puts every tile slightly off.

## 1. The Overview tab

![The Overview tab before an acquisition](/doc/img/overview/overview-tab.png)

- **(1)** The acquisition settings: the beam and its imaging settings, the
  field of view of one tile, the grid of tiles as rows × columns and their
  overlap, and focus options. The scan time is estimated below the imaging
  settings.
- **(2)** Acquire.
- **(3)** The view strip. An overview belongs to a view, which is a beam and
  a stage orientation together, named like "SEM @ SEM". Every view acquired
  this session gets a chip here; click one to see it.
- **(4)** The overviews taken in this session, with their tile count and
  pixel size, and a button to load one from disk.
- **(5)** The lamella positions, the same list as the Experiment tab.

The canvas is drawn in stage coordinates, not image coordinates. Before any
image exists it shows the stage limits as a box and, from the sample holder,
the grid's boundary as a circle, so you can see where an overview will land
before taking it. The toolbar at its top right toggles the tile grid, the
overlays, and contrast and gamma, then the ruler and reset from the other
views.

## 2. Acquire an SEM overview

Choose the electron beam, a tile field of view and the number of tiles.
Three by three at 400 µm covers a millimetre of grid; a whole 3 mm grid at
that field of view is eight by eight. Press **Acquire Overview** and confirm.
The stage visits each tile, the images are placed by their stage positions
and stitched, and the result appears on the canvas.

![A 3 × 3 SEM overview of the grid centre](/doc/img/overview/sem-overview.png)

The stitched overview is a map: grid bars, squares, the film in them, cells
and ice. Zoom and pan as in any view. A single click selects a marker;
a double-click moves the stage to that point, as in the beam views, and
re-images.

## 3. Mark lamella positions

Right-click a spot on the overview and choose **Add New Position Here**. A
lamella is created at that stage position with a generated name, appears in
the list, and is drawn on the overview with its name and a box the size of
the milling field of view.

![Three positions marked on the SEM overview](/doc/img/overview/positions-marked.png)

Click a marker or a row to select a lamella; the move button on its row
drives the stage there. A position is recorded at the orientation the
overview was taken at, which is why the SEM overview is the one to mark on:
the workflow moves each lamella from its SEM position to the milling angle
itself. Removing a lamella from the list removes its marker.

## 4. Overviews with the ion beam

The ion beam can take overviews too, and they are worth having: one at the
SEM orientation shows what the FIB sees of the places you marked, and one
at the MILLING orientation shows the same places as they will look while
milling. Choose the ion beam and acquire at each orientation; each becomes
its own chip in the view strip, named for the orientation the stage was at.

![The ion beam's overview at the SEM orientation](/doc/img/overview/fib-overview-sem-orientation.png)

![The ion beam's overview at the MILLING orientation, one row of tiles](/doc/img/overview/fib-overview-milling-orientation.png)

![The view strip with three views](/doc/img/overview/view-strip.png)

The ion beam looks at the sample from an angle, so its tiles are
foreshortened and step further across the stage than their width. At the
milling angle the beam grazes the surface and a row of tiles spans a long
way, which is why that overview is usually a strip rather than a square.
Positions marked on the SEM overview are drawn on the ion beam's overviews
at the same stage coordinates, so you can check each one before milling.

## 5. The fluorescence overview (Arctis)

On an Arctis the Overview tab has a second modality, **Fluorescence**, with
the same layout: channels instead of beam settings, and an overview taken
with the fluorescence microscope at the FM orientation. It is used to find
labelled cells and mark lamella positions on them, and positions marked
here are projected back onto the SEM overview. This is currently available
on the Arctis only.

![The fluorescence overview tab](/doc/img/overview/fm-overview-tab.png)

- **(1)** The stage must be at the FM orientation with the objective
  inserted; the banner says which is missing, and **(2) Move to FM** takes
  the stage there.
- **(3)** Acquire, with the channels and z-stack settings on the right.

## 6. Correlating SEM Overview and FM Overview

A fluorescence overview taken with another program, such as the METEOR's,
can be laid over an SEM overview and lined up by hand. You can then mark
lamella positions over the fluorescence signal, as in step 3.

Before you start:

- fibsemOS 0.6.0 or newer.
- The FM overview is saved as an OME-TIFF (`.ome.tiff`), the format the
  METEOR's software saves.
- The SEM overview from step 2 is showing, and the grid is loaded on the
  stage. fibsemOS keeps a copy of the imported image in the grid's folder.

Press **(1) Align** in the canvas toolbar. Under **Image**, press
**(2) Load…** and pick the file.

![The Align panel, with Load… under Image](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-align-panel.png)

An image from another program goes through an import dialog first:

![The import dialog for an FM overview from another program](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-import-dialog.png)

Check which axis is channels and which is z (**Swap channel and z** if
they are the wrong way round), the size of a pixel, and each channel's
name and colour. The pixel size and channel names are read from the file
when it has them. Press **Import**.

The image is placed at the centre of the view, at the right size, but not
yet turned or moved to where it was taken. This one is a quarter turn out:

![The FM overview imported over the SEM overview, before it is lined up](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-sem-imported.png)

Press **Fit from points…**. Click a feature on the overview, then the same
feature on the FM image. Grid bar corners, holes and broken film work
well. Make four to six pairs, spread across the image. The RMS at the
bottom says how well they agree. If it says the points fit much better
mirrored, cancel, press **Mirror** in the Align panel and fit again; your
points are kept.

![Fit from points with five matching pairs](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-sem-fit-dialog.png)

Press **Fit and apply**. The image moves, turns and scales onto the
overview:

![The FM overview lined up on the SEM overview](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-sem-fitted.png)

**Align image** lets you drag it, turn it with the handle and scale it from
a corner for a final touch; **Reset** puts it back where it was imported.
The placement is saved with the grid.

## 7. Correlating FIB Overview and FM Overview

The same import works on an ion beam overview, at either orientation. The
fluorescence microscope looks straight down on the grid while the ion beam
looks along it, so fibsemOS foreshortens the FM image to match the view
when it imports it:

![The FM overview imported over the ion beam's overview at the MILLING orientation](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-fib-imported.png)

The points are fitted on the flat sample, not on the screen, so four to
six pairs are still enough at a grazing angle. Click them as in step 6:

![Fit from points on the ion beam's overview](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-fib-fit-dialog.png)

![The FM overview lined up on the ion beam's overview](/doc/img/overview/external-fm-fib-fitted.png)

A correction made in one view carries to the others: an image lined up on
the SEM overview is already in place on the ion beam's.

The import assumes the image was taken looking straight down on the grid,
as the METEOR does. In 0.6.0rc1 that assumption is wrong on a fibsemOS that
has the METEOR configured, and the imported image comes out stretched; use
one without it. If the RMS stays high however many points you add, the
image's shape is wrong, not your points.
