# Grid workflow

Grid screening acquires an SEM and a FIB overview of every grid in the
holder in one run, so that the grids worth a session can be chosen before
any lamella work starts.

By the end of this page the experiment has a named record for every grid
in the holder, each grid has an SEM and a FIB overview, and the grids have
a verdict, a note and a screening report.

## Before you start

- An experiment open. A new experiment's protocol already has the two grid
  tasks this page uses.
- A calibrated holder. On a fixed holder, calibrate each slot on the
  [Sample holder](/docs/sample-holder) page. On an Arctis, calibrate the
  compustage's working slot once, as below.
- On an Arctis, grids in the magazine and the magazine scanned, on the
  Sample tab. A scan takes about 10 minutes with the magazine shut. After
  the magazine is undocked its slots read as unknown until the next scan.

The examples on this page are on the simulated Arctis, with three grids in
the magazine.

### The Arctis working slot

The autoloader puts every grid in the same place: the compustage's working
slot. Until it is calibrated, the working slot is the stage origin, and a
loaded grid sits off the origin in x, y and z, out of coincidence. Grid
tasks centre their overviews on the working slot, so calibrate it once per
instrument: on the Sample tab, press the pencil on the holder panel and
step through to the working slot.

![The calibration wizard at the working slot](/doc/img/grid-workflow/calibrate-working-slot.png)

With a grid loaded, drive the stage until the centre of the grid is under
the electron beam with the beams in coincidence there, then press
**Capture**. The height is captured as well as x and y. **Move to slot**
drives back to the captured position to check it. The calibration is saved
with the microscope configuration and kept across connections.

## 1. Grid tasks

A lamella task is done to one lamella. A **grid task** is done to a whole
grid: it drives to the grid's slot and works from there. Two kinds ship:

- **Beam overview**: a tiled SEM or FIB overview of the grid, as the
  [Overview](/docs/overview) tab takes by hand. One task type serves both
  beams; the beam is one of its settings.
- **Fluorescence overview**, on an instrument with an in-chamber
  fluorescence microscope: moves to the fluorescence microscope, inserts
  the objective, acquires a tiled fluorescence overview, and puts the
  objective back as it found it.

Grid tasks live in their own section of the protocol and are edited on the
Protocol tab, under a **Grid** page beside the lamella tasks.

![The Protocol tab's Grid page with the two shipped grid tasks](/doc/img/grid-workflow/protocol-grid-page.png)

- **(1)** The **Lamella | Grid** selector. Grid shows the grid tasks, and
  the columns to the right follow.
- **(2)** The grid tasks in the protocol. **+** adds one: pick the type and
  give it a name. The name is what the task's outputs are filed under, so
  keep it distinct.
- **(3)** The selected task's settings. A beam overview has an orientation,
  a filename stem and the same tile, imaging and focus settings as the
  Overview tab; a fluorescence overview has channels, tile settings and a
  z-stack. Edits save to the protocol as they are made.

A new experiment's protocol has two grid tasks:

| Task | Beam | Orientation | Tiles | Field of view per tile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SEM Overview | Electron | SEM | 3 x 3 | 500 µm |
| FIB Overview | Ion | FIB | 5 x 5 | 250 µm |

Both are at 1536 x 1024 with 10 % overlap and no autofocus, and neither
depends on the other. There is no fluorescence overview; add one with
**+** on an instrument that has a fluorescence microscope.

### Stage reach

An overview is acquired around its grid's slot, and every tile centre has
to be within the stage's travel. On an Arctis the compustage travels
±377.8 µm in y about the working slot, so the number of rows is usually
the limit. A tile's height is its field of view x 1024 / 1536 at the
default resolution; at a square resolution each row is taller and fewer
fit. The shipped tasks are sized to fit.

When an overview reaches past the stage, the Grid page says so under the
task's title as the overview is sized. Here the SEM overview is set to
4 x 4:

![The reach warning on the Grid page](/doc/img/grid-workflow/protocol-grid-reach.png)

A run acquires the tiles in reach and skips the rest. The run's history,
the Grids tab's results and the screening report name the skipped tiles.

## 2. Grid records

The **Grids** tab, between Lamella and Workflow, holds the experiment's
grid records: one card per grid the experiment knows about, with the
grid's results beside it.

A new experiment has no records. The refresh button under the cards reads
what the hardware holds and adds a record for every grid it finds. On an
Arctis that is what the autoloader found at its last magazine scan; the
refresh does not scan the magazine. On a fixed holder it is the slots
marked occupied.

### Naming

Name the grids before their first run. A grid's name is the name of its
folder, `grids/<grid>/`, and is written into every image it records, so
it is fixed once the grid has run: **Rename…** is greyed out from then on,
with the reason in its tooltip. A correction after a run goes in the
grid's note.

Each card has a menu:

![A grid card's menu](/doc/img/grid-workflow/grid-card-actions.png)

- **Load** and **Unload**, on an Arctis, bring the grid into the working
  slot or send it back to the magazine.
- **Rename…** names the grid.
- **Edit note…** records what was seen; see
  [Verdict and note](#verdict-and-note).
- **Remove** stops tracking the grid. Its files are kept.

Naming a grid on the Sample tab, in a magazine slot or a shuttle slot,
renames its record in the same way, and is refused once the grid has run.

![The Grids tab after the inventory, with the grids named](/doc/img/grid-workflow/grids-tab-inventory.png)

- **(1)** One card per grid. The line under the name is the grid's status:
  the last task completed on it, empty until something has run. A grid in
  the beam carries a **Loaded** chip, and one the hardware no longer
  reports carries **not present**. The icon beside the name is the grid's
  verdict.
- **(2)** The refresh, to run again whenever grids have changed.
- **(3)** The selected grid's results. Nothing has run yet, so there are
  none.

A record keeps its name and history when the grid is taken out of the
instrument; the card reads **not present** until it is back.

## 3. Running the grid tasks

The Workflow tab has a second view beside Lamella: **Grids**. It is the
grid counterpart of the lamella run: pick grids, pick tasks, press Run.

![The Grids view on the Workflow tab](/doc/img/grid-workflow/grids-view.png)

- **(1)** The **Lamella | Grids** selector. Run acts on whichever view is
  showing.
- **(2)** The grids, each with its slot and whether it is present. Tick the
  ones to screen.
- **(3)** The protocol's grid tasks. Tick the ones to run; drag to reorder.
  The order here is the order they run on each grid, and it is saved back
  to the protocol.
- **(4)** **Screen all grids**, covered below.
- **(5)** **Run Workflow**.

The run is grid-outer: it loads the first grid, runs every ticked task on
it, then moves to the next grid. The line above the buttons says how many
exchanges that is. On an Arctis an exchange, unloading one grid and
loading the next, takes about 3 minutes. On a fixed holder every grid is
already in place, so the load step has nothing to do; each task moves the
stage to the grid's slot itself.

Run opens a preflight:

![The preflight for a grid run](/doc/img/grid-workflow/run-preflight.png)

It counts the grids, tasks and exchanges, lists the plan, and says where
the files go: `grids/<grid>/` under the experiment. Two rules of the run
are stated on it: a grid that will not load is skipped and the run
continues with the next, and Stop ends the run at the next step, not
mid-task. It also lists the grids running for the first time, whose names
the run fixes, and says when a beam is off; the run turns it on.

While it runs, the queue on the right shows each grid as an item, with its
load and its tasks as steps:

![The queue mid-run: the first grid done, the second under way](/doc/img/grid-workflow/queue-running.png)

The load is a step of its own, so an exchange that fails is visible as
such. While a run is going, **Add to Queue → Add to End of Queue** adds
more grids to it. A change to a task's settings applies to the grids not
yet run, and a task removed from the protocol is skipped on them.

When the run ends, the summary lists each grid, each task, and how it
went. The last grid stays loaded.

![The run summary for a grid run](/doc/img/grid-workflow/run-summary.png)

Starting a run clears the ticks in the Grids view, so the next run starts
from a fresh selection.

### Screen all grids

**Screen all grids** is the one-button version. It refreshes the
inventory, adds a record for every grid found, and runs the ticked tasks
on all of them, exchanging grids as it goes. On an Arctis the inventory is
the autoloader's last magazine scan, so scan the magazine first; a grid in
a slot that has not been scanned is not screened.

The same preflight opens first. It lists the grids the experiment already
has; a grid that only the inventory finds is added when the run starts.

![The preflight for Screen all grids](/doc/img/grid-workflow/screen-all-preflight.png)

On a fixed holder it is the same run with no exchanges.

## 4. Reviewing the results

Back on the Grids tab, each card carries the thumbnail of its latest
overview and the name of the last task that completed on it. Select a card
to see its history.

![The Grids tab after the run: results for the first grid](/doc/img/grid-workflow/grids-tab-results.png)

- **(1)** The cards, each with its latest overview and its verdict.
- **(2)** The selected grid's history: every load and task, with its
  outcome and the time it ran. An overview that skipped tiles past the
  stage's reach names them here.
- **(3)** The image a task recorded. Click it to open the overview at full
  size, with a scale bar.

![An overview opened from the results](/doc/img/grid-workflow/overview-dialog.png)

The files are under `grids/<grid>/<task>/` in the experiment folder, as
the preflight said: the stitched overview, and a thumbnail beside it.

### Verdict and note

The icon beside a grid's name is its verdict: **Good**, **Rework**,
**Failed** or **Not assessed**. A person sets it, from the icon; no task
does, and the record keeps who set it.

![The verdict menu](/doc/img/grid-workflow/grid-verdict-menu.png)

**Edit note…** on the card's menu records what was seen, in words. The
note shows in the card's tooltip and in the report.

![The note on a grid](/doc/img/grid-workflow/grid-note.png)

### The screening report

**Tools → Reporting → Generate Grid Screening Report** writes
`grid-screening-report.pdf` to the experiment folder and opens it. It
needs the `reporting` extra (`pip install fibsem-os[reporting]`).

The cover names the grid protocol, lists every grid with its slot,
verdict and note, and counts the grids screened, the ones that did not
load, and the ones recommended: those with a verdict of Good.

![The report's cover](/doc/img/grid-workflow/report-cover.png)

Each grid then has a page with its overviews, and an appendix at the end
says how each task ran:

![A grid's page in the report](/doc/img/grid-workflow/report-grid.png)

## 5. Where this fits

Screening comes before anything on the lamella pages: overview every grid
first, then choose which grids are worth the session. On the Grids tab,
the **Positions** view shows a grid's overviews, and a lamella position
marked there belongs to that grid. A lamella run refuses a lamella whose
grid is not on the stage, and names the grid. Milling the positions is the
lamella workflow, from [Overview acquisition](/docs/overview) onward.
